Is Egypt the cheapest? Why 0.2% is not a saving

Data as of 7 November 2026

If you compare Booking.com prices across several countries, Egypt turns up surprisingly often as the “cheapest country”. It sounds like a saving tip. In our data it usually isn’t one: Egypt almost always wins by only a few cents.

What our data shows

From the GeoRates log (16 Sep–7 Nov 2026, all prices including taxes and fees, converted into euros):

  • Egypt was the cheapest country 22 times. In 17 of those cases the advantage was only 0.1% to 0.4%, which is €0.13 to €2.46, median €0.50.
  • Egypt was at least 1% cheaper only 4 times (3.6%, 5.9%, 6.5% and 9.8%). The two cases after our Genius correction on 18 Sep (9.8% and 5.9%) were not stable on the second fetch.
  • Since 19 Sep we have had the Egypt price right next to the German price for 120 checks. Result: in 88 cases (73%) Egypt was within ±1% of the German price, in 20 cases it was at least 1% more expensive, and in 12 cases at least 1% cheaper. Median difference: 0.03%.
  • In 11 of those 12 cases another country was cheaper still than Egypt, for example Peru, India or Argentina.

A typical example from the log: a hotel in Munich cost €164 from Germany and 9,837 EGP from Egypt, which converts to €163.77. GeoRates showed Egypt as the cheapest country, with a difference of €0.23. That is not a saving. It is rounding.

Why Egypt “wins” so often

The likely reason is the conversion. Booking.com shows the price in Egyptian pounds, rounded to a whole amount. Convert that back into euros and the figure sometimes lands a few cents under the German price. With 15 countries compared, it is almost certain that one of them leads by a few cents because of rounding. Peru had the same effect in our data.

That is why GeoRates treats differences under 1% as “no difference”.

When Egypt really is cheaper

When Egypt was clearly cheaper, another country was usually clearly cheaper as well. A plausible explanation: the hotel set a discount for several countries or regions outside the EU (Booking.com for Partners). We do not know that for certain. We only measure what is displayed. For you that means checking Egypt alone is not enough.

Egypt is outside the EU. The EU Geo-blocking Regulation, which forbids price differences by place of residence inside the EU (Verbraucherzentrale), therefore does not apply to Egyptian prices. Country prices are possible, but they rarely show up in our data.

Caveats

  • Conversion costs eat tiny savings: if you pay in Egyptian pounds (EGP), you risk a foreign-currency or conversion fee. With an advantage of a few cents you are almost certainly more expensive in the end.
  • Unstable hits: both of the larger Egypt savings since 19 Sep came out smaller on the second fetch and were marked not stable.
  • More effort, no gain: a VPN, a private window and a new search, just to save a few cents, is not worth it.

How to decide properly

  1. Check your hotel with GeoRates. All 15 countries are compared in the same run.
  2. Ignore differences under 1%. That is usually rounding.
  3. If Egypt is clearly cheaper, look whether another country is cheaper still.
  4. Factor in fees: if you pay in a foreign currency you need a card with no foreign-currency fee, or the saving disappears quickly.

Frequently asked questions

Is Booking.com cheaper from Egypt?

Rarely in any real sense. Egypt was the cheapest country 22 times in our data, but in 17 of those cases only by 0.1% to 0.4%, a few cents up to about €2.50.

Why does the comparison show Egypt so often?

Probably because of rounding when Egyptian pounds are converted into euros. With 15 countries, one of them is almost always a few cents ahead.

From when is another country worth it?

GeoRates only counts differences from 1% up. Whether it is worth it for you also depends on VPN and card fees.

Were there real Egypt savings as well?

Yes, 4 times at least 1%, and 9.8% at most. The two more recent cases were not stable on the second fetch, though.

Does the EU geo-blocking rule apply to Egypt?

No. Egypt is outside the EU, so country prices are allowed there.

Is it legal?

Using a VPN is legal in Germany. You should not book rates that are expressly only for residents.
