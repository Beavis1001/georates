Pay in euros or in local currency? The conversion trap on Booking.com

Data 19 Sep–22 Nov 2026 (GeoRates log), as of 23 Nov 2026

You have found a country on GeoRates where your hotel is cheaper. At payment, Booking offers to charge the amount in euros straight away. That sounds convenient, but it can cost part of your saving.

What Booking says about it

According to the Booking.com terms (section A8, Payment):

  • If the currency you choose is not the property's currency, Booking can show prices in your currency and offer the option “Pay in your own currency” (Pay In Your Own Currency).
  • The fees for that are included in the exchange rate or appear as their own line in the payment step.
  • If you pay in the property's currency, that option does not apply. Your bank or card issuer can then charge fees if your card runs in another currency. Booking points this out during booking.

In short: either Booking converts (the markup is in the rate), or your card converts (the cost depends on your card). With a card that has no foreign-currency fee, the second way is usually cheaper.

What our data shows

From 19 Sep 2026 GeoRates found, in 108 checks, a country that was at least 1% cheaper. The best price was in these currencies:

  • US dollar (USD): 35 times
  • Indian rupee (INR): 28 times
  • Colombian peso (COP): 15 times
  • Japanese yen (JPY) 6, Peruvian sol (PEN), Argentine peso (ARS) and Mexican peso (MXN) 5 each, less often VND, PKR, LKR, EGP (2 each) and PHP (1).

The saving therefore almost always arises in a foreign currency. We convert every currency to euros. When we had to convert the cheapest price from another currency, GeoRates sets a higher threshold than 1%, because Booking sells there at its own rate.

Also: from 19 Sep 2026 a country won 66 times by less than 1%. A conversion eats gaps that small very quickly.

What we do not measure: the rate Booking uses for “Pay in your own currency”, and your card's fees. How much of the advantage is left at the end depends on that.

How to go about it

  1. Check your hotel with GeoRates. The “Local price” column shows the amount in the session's local currency.
  2. Connect to the country that was found and reload the hotel page. The price should match the one that was found.
  3. In the payment step, choose the property's currency, not Booking's euro conversion.
  4. Pay with a card with no foreign-currency fee, that is, no foreign-transaction charge.
  5. For very small savings (under 2–3%), work out whether the effort is worth it.

For countries inside the EU the Geo-blocking Regulation applies: prices there must not differ by origin. The currency question therefore comes up above all for countries outside the EEA.

Caveats

  • Ordinary bank cards often charge a foreign-transaction fee on a foreign currency. Check your card's terms before you book.
  • Paying at the hotel: if you pay only once you are there, the rate on the day you pay applies, not the rate when you booked.
  • Same conditions: room, board, cancellation terms and the final price including taxes have to match.

Frequently asked questions

Should I pay on Booking.com in euros or in local currency?

If you have a card with no foreign-currency fee: in the property's currency. With “Pay in your own currency”, Booking's markup is in the exchange rate according to the terms, or it appears as its own line.

What is “Pay in your own currency” on Booking?

A Booking service where you pay in your home currency and Booking converts. The fees are included in the rate or listed separately in the payment step.

In which currency are country prices usually cheaper?

In our data from 19 Sep 2026, most often in US dollars (35 times), rupees (28 times) and Colombian pesos (15 times).

Is a country price with a 1% saving worth it?

Usually not. Even small conversion costs can wipe out the advantage.
